# Five-year analyst evaluation

Requested by Dirk on 10 September 2026. Target companies: Broadcom, Alphabet, NVIDIA, SK hynix, Samsung Electronics, Micron, Sandisk and ASML.

## Target result

Identify the analysts whose earnings forecasts were most useful for each company over roughly the last five years. The result should show forecast accuracy, performance against contemporaneous consensus, sample size, coverage gaps, recent performance and current coverage status. A candidate list is a collection priority, not a completed accuracy ranking.

Use outcomes announced from 10 September 2021 through 10 September 2026. Request estimates from September 2020 onward so that one-year-ahead forecasts for the beginning of the outcome window are not discarded. Retain later unresolved forecasts for the live research page, but exclude them from accuracy scoring.

For Sandisk, the current standalone equity began regular trading on 24 February 2025. Keep its shorter company history explicit. A forecaster's earlier Western Digital memory research can be supporting experience but cannot be scored as a forecast of the same standalone company's EPS. [Issuer separation announcement](https://investor.sandisk.com/news-releases/news-release-details/sandisk-celebrates-nasdaq-listing-after-completing-separation).

## Evidence levels

| Evidence | Permitted conclusion |
|---|---|
| Matched, dated stock-specific EPS/revenue forecasts versus comparable actuals and contemporaneous consensus | Measured forecasting performance for that company, metric, horizon and sample |
| Provider's stock-specific estimate-accuracy score, with date and methodology | External accuracy evidence for its defined window; not automatically our custom five-year score |
| Quantitative sector earnings-estimator award | Useful candidate-selection evidence; does not prove best performance on an individual company |
| Award combining forecast accuracy with price-target performance | Mixed-method candidate-selection evidence; do not relabel as a pure EPS ranking |
| Client vote, reputation survey, price-target/recommendation returns, or self-selected successful calls | Coverage/reputation or stock-selection evidence only |
| Public report or issuer coverage list | The analyst covers the company; accuracy remains unknown |

## Historical population

Request all contributors who covered each company during the period, including analysts who stopped coverage. Restricting the input to today's visible analysts can conceal poor or discontinued records. Current coverage status can subsequently determine who is useful to follow now.

Maintain person identity separately from broker affiliation and team authorship. Record dated firm changes; do not assume a familiar surname identifies the same person, or count two coauthors as independent forecasts. Preserve missing or masked identities and state the limitation. A successor's current model should not inherit a predecessor's track record without attribution.

Keep stock splits, currency and per-share bases consistent between forecasts and actuals. Preserve the original provider field labels. Adjusted versus unadjusted share data and GAAP versus non-GAAP accounting are different dimensions.

## Initial comparison rules

These are proposed research rules, not a backtested scoring system.

- Compare quarterly EPS and revenue separately. Start at 90 days before the actual earnings announcement; inspect 180-day forecasts separately. Compare annual forecasts at 180 and 365 days separately.
- At each cutoff, use the latest estimate actually available then. Record forecast age and missing coverage. Do not import a later revision or assume a report's model date is its public dissemination date.
- Match the analyst and consensus to the same fiscal target, cutoff, currency and accounting/share basis. Prefer consensus excluding the analyst being assessed when the contributor panel is available; disclose if it includes them.
- Report absolute errors, signed bias, and paired wins/losses against consensus. Avoid percentage EPS errors near zero. Do not blend operating profit, EPS and revenue into one unexplained score.
- Show the full five-year result, each year and the most recent two years. Use common company-period comparisons where possible, so one analyst is not rewarded for covering only easier periods. Keep shorter records visible.
- Use at least 12 comparable quarterly outcomes across multiple years as an initial eligibility threshold for a provisional ranking. This threshold is a research choice, not proof of reliability. Annual-only records over five years will be small and should retain prominent sample counts.
- Report uncertainty and do not force a winner where differences are small or coverage is weak. A top-three group may be better supported than a precise first place.

To test whether selection improves future forecasts, choose analysts on an earlier portion of the record and evaluate later untouched outcomes. Re-selecting the best analyst using all five years and then reporting their performance on those same years is a descriptive ranking, not evidence of a tradable historical selection rule.
